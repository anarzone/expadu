Design a completely new, adaptive Today experience for **Expadu**, a daily companion for people living in a new city.

**Be brave. Take creative ownership. Preserve the product's purpose and useful capabilities, but invent a substantially different design.**

The goal is an exceptionally user-friendly, fun and interactive application that people enjoy returning to. Make it feel considered, expressive and alive, with immediate clarity about what matters and what to do next.

Deliver a high-fidelity design proposal for review. Do not implement application code yet.

**A fresh start — read this before designing**

Previous Expadu screens and proposals are context about the product, not templates to reproduce. The previous prompt over-specified the palette, staggered dashboard, navigation, connected overview panel and card treatments. Those visual prescriptions are deliberately released for this exploration.

This brief replaces the earlier visual brief. Existing design-system constraints are not requirements for this separate concept. The current app remains unchanged until a new direction is approved.

You have freedom to rethink:

- Information architecture: how Today groups information and reveals detail.
- Navigation: position, presentation and how people move between capabilities.
- Composition: reading order, spatial relationships and use of screen space.
- Visual identity: a distinctly new palette, typography and graphic language.
- Components: cards, rows, controls, surfaces and alternatives to those patterns.
- Interactions: how people explore, act, disclose information and reach Composer.
- Motion: meaningful feedback, transitions and expressive moments.

Keep the name Expadu. The old logo treatment, font and colours are not visual anchors; a simple wordmark is sufficient if a separate logo exploration would distract from the page.

Do not reproduce the old pale-blue canvas, white rounded panels, cyan selections and orange actions as the default recipe. Choose and justify a fresh palette. Familiar semantic colours can still serve their normal purpose.

Do not trace the old four-part overview, staggered card grid, hero-plus-two-supporting-cards arrangement, five-item header strip or floating assistant button. None of these structures is mandatory. The same capabilities may have a much better home in a different composition.

Changing only colours, photos, corner radii or fonts is not enough. The new experience must differ in its structure, hierarchy and interaction design as well.

If previous screenshots are available, use them only to understand capabilities and known problems. Do not feed them into image generation as style or layout references. Build the visual direction from this brief.

**The product ideas to preserve**

Expadu helps people answer:

“What matters to me today, and what can I do next?”

It connects personal plans, events, journeys, paperwork and services through an assistant called Composer. Cologne is the first city; the design must work in other cities too.

People come with different intentions: get somewhere, keep an appointment, prepare paperwork, discover a city event or simply understand their day. Some are older, new to the city, unfamiliar with local administration or using the app for the first time.

Preserve access to these needs without assuming that each deserves its own permanent box on Today. You can combine, sequence or progressively reveal information when this improves understanding.

Events provide enjoyment and discovery. Personal commitments and relevant changes establish urgency. The page needs both practical value and personality.

Places are supporting context for venues, journeys and plans. Do not make standalone place discovery the main attraction or add it back as a primary destination.

**An adaptive experience with three situations**

Design one coherent system that handles all three situations. These are real content states, not three unrelated themes.

1. Everyday

   Lead with useful personal orientation: the next relevant plan or action, journey information when useful, recorded paperwork progress and a small amount of useful weather.

   If nothing is scheduled, give the user a welcoming, useful starting point. Keep discovery and practical capabilities accessible. Do not manufacture urgency or leave an empty dashboard.

   Event discovery comes after the useful overview. It should still feel appealing and worth exploring.

2. A significant event

   A relevant city festival, concert, sporting occasion, film event or other exceptional highlight can earn the most prominent visual position.

   Find an original way to give the event presence while keeping the person's day easy to reach. You are not required to use a conventional hero, a split layout or a carousel.

   Promote the event once. Do not repeat it in another discovery section. Keep other discoveries available.

3. A relevant personal disruption

   A delay on the person's usual route becomes the leading item within their personal overview.

   Communicate the route, delay, scheduled time, expected time and a clear way to check alternatives. Composer can help with the affected journey.

   Make the personal overview adapt naturally. Its exact structure and visual treatment are yours to design. Preserve access to other plans, paperwork, weather and discovery.

An active problem affecting an existing commitment takes precedence over general entertainment. Unrelated disruptions, expired news and ordinary recommendations should not take over the page.

When several things matter, establish one clear lead and a readable secondary hierarchy. Adapt on entry or refresh; keep the page stable while someone reads or interacts. Background updates must not unpredictably rearrange the interface.

**Design around actions**

The most important capabilities are:

- Understand the next commitment and open the relevant agenda.
- Check a journey or find an alternative.
- Continue a specific paperwork process.
- Discover an event and open its details.
- Save an idea for later.
- Add an activity to a proposed plan.
- Ask Composer for help with the item already in context.
- Reach the wider Events, journeys/departures, Bureaucracy and Services sections.

Preserve these capabilities and connections. Their placement, grouping and presentation are open for redesign.

Prefer direct interactions and short routes to useful outcomes. Reduce unnecessary choices, duplicate entry points and generic “Learn more” buttons. Each component should have an understandable main action.

Use concise nouns, times, status and action labels. Avoid paragraphs explaining how to use the page. A little personality in the copy is welcome when it adds clarity or delight.

**Paperwork: understandable at first glance**

A user must be able to identify a process, understand recorded progress and open that exact process.

The earlier design mixed a navigation arrow, previous/next arrows and a carousel counter. Do not repeat that ambiguity.

Invent a clear treatment with direct access to the relevant process and an understandable route to any additional processes. A list is allowed, but it is not a prescribed component. Choose the structure that best supports the task.

Do not hide the important next step behind a carousel. A document-progress count must describe actual recorded progress; a pagination count must never compete with it.

Use only supplied or verified paperwork facts. Do not invent requirements, fees, deadlines or legal advice. Mock progress is illustrative and must be identified as sample content.

**Events should feel alive**

Make the city feel worth exploring. Use strong photography or suitable artwork with atmosphere, people and activity. Represent more than one kind of enjoyment: music, sport, games, film, exhibitions, outdoor activities and celebrations.

Design an integrated event experience. Find an original relationship between image, title, date, location and action. Avoid falling back to a generic photograph above a large white information block and oversized promotional button.

Use typography, crop, rhythm and colour with intent. Titles and essential timings must stay readable with bright, dark or visually busy images.

Save and Plan are different actions:

- Save keeps an idea for later.
- Plan opens a proposed activity for the person to review.
- Opening details is a third, distinct interaction.

Keep those meanings and prevent accidental activation. The shape, placement and treatment of the controls are open. Glass is an option, not a requirement.

Most events have fixed start and end times. Reflect the supplied schedule. Offer visit-time choices only when the event explicitly supports flexible attendance.

Provide an attractive treatment when an image is unavailable. Never imply that an unrelated or generated scene documents the real event. Use approved media or clearly labelled illustrative assets.

**Fun and interactive must mean something**

Create delight through useful interaction, expressive imagery, confident composition and satisfying feedback.

Show how at least three meaningful interactions work. For example, demonstrate choosing a next action, exploring an event, revealing relevant information or carrying a route into Composer. Select the interactions that best explain your design.

For each, show:

- The resting state.
- The user's action.
- The visible response and resulting state.
- A clear way back or an undo where relevant.

Prefer interactions that feel immediate, natural and easy to repeat. Give touch actions feedback as thoughtfully as mouse actions.

Use smooth, short, interruptible motion. Respect reduced motion. Keep important actions visible and usable without animation or hover.

Exploration can feel playful. Tasks involving paperwork, travel disruption or commitments should feel reassuring and easy to complete. Do not introduce points, streaks, mandatory tours or decorative mini-games simply to make the app “fun.”

Retain safe removal behavior for planned activities: swipe left or double-tap reveals a compact red Delete action beside the item; revealing it does not delete anything. Provide an accessible non-gesture route and an undo after deletion. Keep the item recognizable during the reveal.

**Composer belongs in the experience**

Composer connects the user's intention to relevant content and action. Choose a natural place and interaction for it. You do not have to reproduce the existing floating sparkle button or a large chat panel.

People should understand when Composer can help and how to reach it. Direct navigation must remain available for straightforward tasks; do not make everyone talk to an assistant to use the app.

A contextual entry carries the specific route, event, paperwork process or day into an editable request. Preserve any existing draft and conversation. Give the person control over sending and confirming changes.

Saving an event is not planning it. Opening Composer must not silently send a message. A proposed activity is not a confirmed agenda item.

Global search remains a later feature. Do not make this concept depend on implementing a new search system. Keep the architecture capable of accommodating it later.

**Mobile is the primary design**

Start with a typical 390 × 844 phone viewport and account for a narrow 320px screen.

The first screen should contain useful information and its relevant action. Avoid letting navigation, greetings, decoration or a giant image consume the whole initial viewport.

Use readable text, comfortable touch targets and clear scrolling behavior. Keep important controls within easy reach. Ensure a newcomer can tell what is interactive.

Choose the best way to show event discovery on mobile; a horizontal rail is not mandatory. Avoid duplicated event collections and cramped, information-heavy columns.

Make full use of desktop space around 1440px without stretching the phone layout into a large empty page. The desktop composition should feel deliberately designed, while retaining the same interaction meanings.

Show the complete mobile reading order and enough desktop content to evaluate the page beyond its first screen.

**Visual freedom with usability discipline**

Choose a distinct, coherent visual language. You may explore a different light, dark or mixed treatment, a new typographic personality, unconventional but clear composition, and expressive material or graphic details.

Be ambitious about the page's personality. Make intentional decisions instead of assembling familiar dashboard templates.

Freedom of appearance must support:

- Clear hierarchy and immediate orientation.
- Readable contrast and text.
- Consistent icon meanings.
- Obvious selected, saved, pressed and disabled states.
- Comfortable touch targets and visible keyboard focus.
- Predictable navigation and a clear way back.
- Content that remains usable without photography or motion.

Use one coherent icon family. Prefer familiar symbols when they communicate an action quickly; keep a short label when an icon would make people guess. A tooltip is not sufficient explanation for an unfamiliar mobile control.

Prefer separation through spacing, tone, shape or depth over repeated hard black outlines. Keep sticky actions from obscuring content. Maintain focus and reading position when details open.

Do not claim accessibility or usability has been tested if you are presenting static images. Explain any behavior that needs validation during implementation.

**Fresh users and uncertain information**

New users enter the app immediately. Settlement questions and guided help are optional and contextual.

Design useful empty, loading, unavailable and stale states. Missing data should not cause the entire page to disappear.

Distinguish saved ideas, proposals, confirmed plans and recorded progress. Never imply that sample information is live. Keep dates and times coherent within each scenario.

Use one unobtrusive sample disclosure where it is clear enough; label simulated alerts and illustrative imagery explicitly. Do not overwhelm every component with repeated disclaimers.

**How to approach the design**

First, identify the few jobs the user should accomplish from Today. Explore materially different structural approaches before choosing one. Do not let the first familiar dashboard pattern become the answer.

Select one strong direction and develop it across all three situations. Take responsibility for the visual decisions; the brief is intentionally open so you can create something better.

Check the proposal against the previous design:

- If the colours and images were removed, would the composition still be clearly different?
- Have the information grouping and interaction patterns improved?
- Would a newcomer understand the next action without explanation?
- Does the design feel enjoyable while practical tasks remain effortless?

If the result is essentially the old layout with a new skin, redesign it before presenting.

**Deliverables**

Present one new visual direction in six high-fidelity views:

- Everyday: mobile and desktop.
- Important event: mobile and desktop.
- Route disruption: mobile and desktop.

Include an interaction sheet or storyboard showing the most meaningful flows, including distinct event Save/Plan actions, direct access to a paperwork process and contextual Composer behavior.

When the output is static, illustrate before/after states and annotate intended motion. Do not describe a static board as a working interactive prototype.

Use realistic, coherent sample content. Make the screens detailed enough to judge usability, not just mood boards.

Briefly explain:

- The central design idea.
- The most important structural departures from the old experience.
- How the three situations change priority.
- Where the fun and interactivity improve the user's experience.

Keep that explanation short. Let the design demonstrate the direction.

**Success criteria**

Within five seconds, a newcomer should be able to identify what matters now and a useful next action.

Practical information should be approachable. Discovery should feel inviting. The page should work for a first-time user with no agenda and a returning user with a busy day.

The three situations should feel like one adaptive product, while this new product direction is visibly and structurally different from the current Expadu screens.

Be bold enough to surprise us, and clear enough that nobody has to learn how to use it.
