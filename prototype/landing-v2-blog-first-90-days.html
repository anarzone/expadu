The ordered newcomer checklist

Your first 90 days in Cologne, in order.

German paperwork is a dependency chain. This is the sequence that keeps one missing document from blocking the next three steps.

10 min readBy Anar · expat in CologneOfficial sources checked 13 July 2026
On this pageBefore you flyDays 1–14Weeks 1–3Weeks 2–8Months 1–3After 90 days

Most newcomer checklists are a pile of tasks. But the order matters: your registered address drives official mail; health-insurance evidence matters for work, study and many residence routes; permit timing depends on your current status.

Before travel

Secure identity and civil-status documents, translations where needed, and an address you can actually register.

Days 1–14

Register your address and protect the documents produced by the appointment.

Weeks 1–8

Connect health insurance, tax, banking and any necessary residence application.

Months 1–3

Handle household obligations, insurance decisions and the transit setup that fits your routine.

Before you fly

  • Check passport validity. A short remaining validity can limit the period of a visa or residence document.
  • Bring the originals that are hard to replace. Depending on your route: birth and marriage certificates, qualifications, admission or employment documents, and financial evidence.
  • Check translation and legalisation requirements. Do not assume an English original is accepted for every procedure.
  • Line up a registrable address. A signed lease alone is not the same as the Wohnungsgeberbestätigung needed for Anmeldung.
Your situation changes the document pack.

A student, employee, EU citizen, Blue Card holder and accompanying family do not share one universal checklist. Use the authority page for your exact route.

Days 1–14: register the address

Register after moving into the home. You normally need identity documents and the separate confirmation from the housing provider. Cologne's service page is the current source for booking, forms and local requirements.

Read our complete English Anmeldung guide before the appointment. It covers the landlord-form trap and what to keep afterwards.

The dependency this unlocks

Your registered address becomes the delivery point for official mail, including the tax identification number after a first registration in Germany.

Weeks 1–3: health, tax and money

1. Make your health-insurance status explicit

Health insurance is mandatory in Germany, but the right path depends on whether you are employed, studying, self-employed, privately insured or covered through family. Ask the insurer for the evidence your employer, university or authority needs.

2. Watch for your tax ID

After your first registration, the Federal Central Tax Office sends the tax ID to your registered address. You keep this number for life. If it does not arrive, use the BZSt process rather than applying for a “new” number.

3. Choose banking for the life you actually have

Account-opening requirements vary by bank. Compare identity checks, fees, cash access, language support and whether your employer or landlord needs a particular account function. Do not let an influencer's referral code choose your financial setup.

Weeks 2–8: residence status, if you need it

EU/EEA citizens do not need a German residence permit for free-movement residence. Other newcomers should work backwards from the expiry and conditions of their current visa or status.

  • Identify the exact route—employment, EU Blue Card, study, family or self-employment.
  • Use Cologne's official immigration-office instructions for the correct application channel and document list.
  • Submit in time. Do not assume a distant appointment date and a submitted application are legally the same thing.
  • Keep proof of submission and read any interim document for its precise work and travel conditions.

For orientation: the 2026 EU Blue Card salary thresholds are route-specific and updated annually. Use the current federal portal, not an old blog screenshot.

Months 1–3: the quiet obligations

Broadcast contribution

The Rundfunkbeitrag is organised per dwelling, not per person. When a household member already pays, you normally provide that contribution number rather than creating a second payment. Check the current official amount and rules directly.

Personal liability insurance

Private Haftpflicht is not generally mandatory, but it covers a category of everyday liability risk that can become expensive quickly. Compare exclusions, coverage limits and whether partners or children are included.

Transit

The Deutschlandticket costs €63 per month in 2026 and covers participating local and regional public transport nationwide—not long-distance ICE, IC or EC services. Use the local break-even calculator with your real weekly trips.

After 90 days: build the longer plan

Once the urgent dependencies are stable, record the dates that matter later: passport expiry, permit expiry, qualification milestones and the start of a residence track. The permanent-residence threshold differs by route.

Our permanent-residence timeline gives you the date math and, just as importantly, shows what the calculator cannot verify.

The honest caveat

This order is a framework, not your personal legal decision tree. Family, citizenship, employment, benefits, previous residence and the exact permit can move or add steps.
