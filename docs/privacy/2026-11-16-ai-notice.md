# AI Act Art. 50(1): telling people Composer is an AI (16 Nov 2026)

**Status:** needs the owner's decision (track, week 3, "Legal minimum"). It touches the owner's rule "no AI wording in the app outside Settings". The track already names this notice as the one legally required exception, to be confirmed.

## What the law asks

AI Act Art. 50(1) has applied since **2 Aug 2026**: a system meant to interact directly with people must be designed so that they are told they are dealing with an AI, unless that is obvious from the context. The AI Omnibus didn't delay it. Fines go up to €15M or 3% of turnover. Sources are in `docs/personalisation/2026-11-16-evidence-market.md` §6.

## What Expadu does today

**iPhone**
- The line under Composer's box reads: *"Composer sends what you type, with your nearest stop and neighbourhood, to Anthropic PBC to answer you · Privacy notice · Withdraw"* (`ComposerScreen.notice`).
- It names the processor, not that Composer is an AI. "Anthropic PBC" doesn't tell most people that.
- Composer's settings say the same.

**Web**
- The consent line next to the box says the same.
- The privacy policy says "Some features use Claude, an AI service".

**Assessment:** probably not "obvious from context". Composer looks like a chat, but so do human support chats.

## Options

| | What | Where it shows | Against the no-AI-words rule? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A (recommended)** | Change the existing line under the box to: *"Composer is an AI assistant (Claude, by Anthropic). It gets what you type, with your nearest stop and neighbourhood · Privacy notice · Withdraw"* | Where the line is now, always | Only this one line, which is already there |
| B | A one-time card on the first open: *"Composer is an AI assistant. It can be wrong; times, places and fees come from Expadu's own checked data."* plus "Got it". The line stays as now | Once, then in Settings | One card, once |
| C | A + B | Both | Both |

**Why A.**
- It adds no new element, changes words that are already there, and is visible at every message, which is the moment Art. 50(1) is about.
- B alone leaves later sessions without the notice.
- The "It can be wrong" sentence from B can go into Composer's settings without breaking the rule.

**Not changed by this:** answers stay unlabelled sentence by sentence. Today's day line and the ideas' "why" lines are written by code, not by the AI, so they need no notice.

## Art. 50(2), for the lawyer

Art. 50(2) is about marking AI-generated content in a machine-readable way. It falls on *providers* of systems that generate synthetic text. Expadu builds Composer on Claude, so Expadu may count as a provider of the Composer system. Systems on the market before 2 Aug 2026 have until **2 Dec 2026**.

The question for the lawyer is whether Composer's short answers in an app need machine-readable marking, or whether that applies only to content published to others.

## When decided

The owner picks A, B or C. It is then a one-line change in `ComposerScreen.notice` and the web's consent line (A), or a small card (B), followed by the usual simulator check.
